# Process Overview

## 1 From the Brief to an Idea

The brief asks for a multi-user, real-time website that is good, and leaves what "good" means to me. On the morning of the crit 8 cutoff I had a deployed placeholder and no idea; `/comp4020:ship` stopped at preflight because nothing was built. I asked the agent for candidates and then for an adversarial comparison, and kept asking until about twenty-five ideas had been argued against. Its first ranking judged them by what could be live by 07:00 and put a shared lamp first. I said the final artefact mattered more than the cutoff, so it reread the crit 9 and 10 specs and the marking: ten minutes of a marker's use, a real decision about several people acting at once, and logs with a story to tell. The lamp fell to last. I rejected the step sequencer it then recommended, because my crit 4 project was already an instrument, and chose Fridge Poetry over an island-mapping game. The rule that makes it more than a magnet toy, that each word exists once, came from comparing it with a letterpress idea where the type runs out. Every later decision is tested against that rule.

## 2 Stack and Harness

I chose plain Node with no dependencies and one JSON file on the Fly volume ([ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-plain-node-and-a-json-file.md)). The state is about a hundred positions, so crit 7's SQLite and migrations would have been machinery without a job, and a single process owning all state made server-sent events straightforward later. The cost is that every move rewrites the file, which is fine for a room of people.

The harness has three parts that are meant to agree. `README.md` argues what good means and lists which claims are enforced and which are judged. `CLAUDE.md`, carried over from crit 7 and cut to the rules this app needs, turns those claims into rules: the server never accepts text, positions are fractions of the door, a theft notice never names the taker. `spec/` holds the testable claims against the running app, 17 checks across six files. From the real-time layer onwards every feature was built test-first: each new spec file failed for the expected reason before the code existed, and commits landed only on green. Decisions that are expensive to reverse are in `docs/adr/`.

## 3 How I Directed the Agent

My working pattern was to have the agent compare options without choosing, make the choice myself, and then have it record the reasoning. That is how the idea, the crit 9 decision and the archive were settled. Between features I ran `/simplify` and `/code-review` over the agent's own work, and required a check in a real browser after each change, because several of the faults below were invisible to the tests. The visual direction was mine as well: I asked for the pages to be beautified ([`976e919`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/976e919)), said the result still did not look like a fridge, which led to the cabinet, freezer door and feet ([`6250c54`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/6250c54)), and later asked for a white fridge and dark mode ([`dd78765`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/dd78765)).

## 4 Corrections

The first version ([`3baa0c1`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/3baa0c1)) passed its tests, but dragging "moon" moved "lost", because starting words were stacked on top of each other. Laying them out in rows hid the fault only on first load. The `/simplify` review found the cause, that magnets stacked in vocabulary order, and [`03e99c4`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/03e99c4) put the last-moved word on top with a spec check on the order. `/code-review` then showed that this refactor had made things worse: it shifted every word already on the live door and reshuffled the Tab order. Rather than patch each symptom, [`6d4bfdb`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/6d4bfdb) separated the two orders, keeping the DOM in vocabulary order and stacking by z-index. A fix written against a symptom produced the next bug, and only the review that asked for the cause stopped the chain.

Browser checks caught what no test could. At phone width, 137 pairs of starting words overlapped because positions scaled with the door while the font stopped at 11px; [`640575b`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/640575b) sizes words as a fraction of the door. The notice line sat below the fridge, at 1175px in a 900px window, so no notice had ever been seen; it now floats over the screen ([`c76fed4`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/c76fed4)), and `CLAUDE.md` carries that as a rule. For the archive, the server has to estimate how wide a word is, so the agent measured every magnet in the browser before relying on its formula: the first ran up to 29% wide and would have missed words placed touching, and the fitted one is within 0.66em ([`b6dcdab`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/b6dcdab)). `CLAUDE.md` requires refitting it if the magnets' font changes.

## 5 Several People at Once

Crit 9 asks for real-time behaviour and one written decision about it. [`39fc956`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/39fc956) streams every move to every open page over server-sent events; `spec/realtime.test.ts` holds the brief's one-second bound, and on the live site a drag reached a second browser in 28ms. The decision was what happens when two people grab the same word. The agent set out three options: last to let go wins, which the real-time layer had left by default; one hand at a time; and snatching a word from someone's hand. I chose one hand at a time, because it extends the central rule to how a real magnet behaves: only one person can hold it. [ADR 0002](docs/adr/0002-one-hand-at-a-time.md) records what that costs, mainly that a hold must end even when its holder disappears, and [`f8464fb`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/f8464fb) implements it, with `spec/hold.test.ts` covering each refusal and each way a hold ends.

After crit 8 I also said the app was too simple. Of the agent's six proposals I took the three that sharpen the central rule rather than adding new claims: one hand at a time, notices when someone takes your word, which never name the taker ([`c76fed4`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/c76fed4)), and an archive of broken lines, so that taking a word costs a line but not the poem ([ADR 0003](docs/adr/0003-an-archive-of-broken-lines.md)). I left out timed slam rounds, which would have pulled the app towards a game whose argument the README does not make.

## 6 What Is Still Open

The archive's thresholds, three words and fifteen seconds, are the agent's proposal and still open. Every test that changes the door now skips the live one and runs only against CI's throwaway copy, leaving read-only checks live ([`b5901ea`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-petrewei/commit/b5901ea)). The README cites the brief and the week 8 lecture; the small-web writing the brief points to is not yet read, so my position on it is not here yet. Crit 10 adds server-side logging.
